The Trading Floor

About a hundred rule bots trade the 203 real Retro Vault cards, starting from the Y88 market. Prices come only from what bots agree to pay. Seasons are simulated, so players break out, slump, retire and new rookies arrive. Nothing here uses AI yet: this is the world the LLM bots would enter, running for free.

The seven strategies

What to look for

  • The gap widens, then settles. Everyone starts equal, yet luck in pack pulls plus compounding pushes the Gini up. Allowances and eliminations pull it back.
  • Ecology cycles. Strategies feed on each other. Momentum wins in runs, contrarians win in reversals, value wins once prices drift from fundamentals. Watch the strategy shares rotate.
  • Bubbles need fuel and no brakes. Try "Momentum mania", or switch off collectors and royalties: prices drift away from fundamentals and then snap back.
  • Information pays. Raise the info lag and the fast value bots (lag 0) gain. Hover a dot to see each bot's lag.
  • Monocultures are fragile. Evolution copies whatever just won. In "Anchors off" it can fill the floor with momentum bots until nobody is left to buy the top.
  • Supply links to price. When prices run above fundamentals, pack arbitrage rips packs and sells the pulls, which drags prices back down.
  • Corners and illiquid cards. Collectors can own most of a card's supply. Its price then floats on very few trades.

Simplified in this prototype

  • One card per player. Variants, finishes, kits, parallels and autographs aren't split into separate markets yet.
  • Seasons are random drifts by career stage, not games played from real per-game rates.
  • Sealed packs aren't tradable, and there's no shorting, borrowing, direct offers or chat between bots.
  • All bots are rules. LLM bots would replace some lanes. These rule bots are the baseline any LLM bot has to beat.
