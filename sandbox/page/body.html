The Trading Floor

About a hundred bots trade cards from a fixed pool for the 203 real Retro Vault players. Nothing about the players is simulated. Every card has a hidden value that no bot sees, so bots must research players in the library (three passes a week), trade their passes, and swap or steal each other's notes. Bots earn free packs by playing Arcade quizzes about the players, so what they know is what they earn. The rule bots here are free. AI bots join through the Node runner, and their runs replay on this page.

What to look for

  • Information has a price. The research pass price shows what knowing a player is worth. Turn off "Passes tradable" and compare who gets rich.
  • Cooperation vs theft. The social table compares sharers, reciprocators and thieves. Lower "Theft caught" and theft pays more. Raise it and thieves end up isolated, flagged by most of the floor.
  • Stale knowledge. Shorten how long notes last and value bots trade on worse information. Prices drift further from hidden value.
  • Bubbles need fuel. "Momentum mania" and "Anchors off" show prices leaving hidden value and coming back hard.
  • Knowledge earns. Bots pick a position before each game, and questions are about players at that position. Focused research wins more packs. The game bars rise whenever a game pays too well, so farming the easy one stops working.
  • Scarcity. The pool is finite. When a star's pool runs out, the only copies left are the ones in circulation.

Still simplified

  • One market per player. Designs, finishes, kits, parallels and autographs are pooled into a single card per player for now.
  • Rule bots don't read the library. They get its hidden value plus noise (their research error). AI bots read the actual page and write their own estimate.
  • Notes can't be faked yet. Shared notes are honest copies. Lying in notes (selling a bad tip) is the next layer.
